Decompositions

MIMIQ provides a flexible, rule-based framework for decomposing quantum operations into simpler instruction sets. This is essential for transpilation to hardware constraints, converting circuits between different standards (e.g., OpenQASM, Clifford+T), and optimizing circuits.

Core Functions

The primary entry points are decompose and decompose!.

Decompose

decompose creates a new circuit where all operations are terminal in the target basis.

# Decompose to the default canonical basis (basic primitive gates)
new_circuit = decompose(circuit)

# Decompose a specific operation into a circuit of primitives
sub_circuit = decompose(GateSWAP())

In-place Decomposition

decompose! appends decomposed instructions to an existing circuit or collection.

# Decompose source and append to target circuit
decompose!(target_circuit, source_circuit)

# Decompose operation and append to target circuit
decompose!(target_circuit, GateH())

Single-step Decomposition

decompose_step applies one rewrite rule once, without recursing into the result. See Step-wise decomposition below.

Decomposition Bases

Decomposition behavior is defined by a DecompositionBasis. A basis determines:

  1. Terminal Operations: Which instructions are allowed (supported limits).
  2. Decomposition Logic: How to transform unsupported operations into supported sequences.

You can specify a basis when calling decompose:

# Decompose to a specific basis
decompose(circuit, basis=CliffordTBasis())

Built-in Bases

MIMIQ includes several built-in decomposition bases:

BasisDescription
CanonicalBasis(Default) Decomposes to a standard set of primitive gates (U, CX, Measure, etc.).
QASMBasisTargets the OpenQASM 2.0 gate library (qelib1.inc).
CliffordTBasisDecomposes to the Clifford+T universal gate set: the operations of StimBasis plus T and T†.
StimBasisTargets the operations of the Stim stabilizer simulator (version 1.16): Clifford gates, Pauli-basis and Pauli-product measurements, Pauli noise, and Paulis conditioned on one measured bit.
FlattenedBasisFlattens all container operations (blocks, calls) while preserving gates.
# Example: Decompose to QASM 2.0 gates
qasm_circ = decompose(circuit, basis=QASMBasis())

Clifford+T and gridsynth

CliffordTBasis rewrites a circuit into Clifford and T gates. Rotations by multiples of $\pi/4$ are decomposed exactly. Other rotations are approximated by GridSynthRewrite, which implements the Ross-Selinger gridsynth algorithm, by default to an operator-norm error of $10^{-10}$ each. The rotations keyword selects a different tolerance or rule:

c = push!(Circuit(), GateRZ(0.123), 1)

# approximate the rotation to 1e-10 with Clifford+T gates
fine = decompose(c; basis=CliffordTBasis())

# a looser tolerance gives a shorter circuit
shorter = decompose(c; basis=CliffordTBasis(rotations=GridSynthRewrite(epsilon=1e-4)))

length(shorter), length(fine)
(110, 255)

CliffordTBasis(sk_depth=3) uses the Solovay-Kitaev algorithm at recursion depth 3 instead, as earlier versions did by default.

The terminal operations of CliffordTBasis are those of StimBasis plus GateT and GateTDG, classical operations, and any conditional of its terminal operations. Measurements and resets in every Pauli basis, Pauli noise and Pauli rotations by $\pm\pi/2$ are therefore kept as they are, not expanded into elementary gates.

Conditional operations

CliffordTBasis, FlattenedBasis and RuleBasis decompose the operation inside an IfStatement with their own rules and put each resulting instruction under the same condition, so a classically controlled rotation decomposes like an unconditioned one. StimBasis keeps a GateX, GateY or GateZ conditioned on one bit being 1, which is Stim's feedback CX rec[-k] q. It rewrites the same gate conditioned on 0 as the gate followed by that conditional, and throws a DecompositionError for any other conditional operation.

Rewrite Rules

A basis is built from rewrite rules. A RewriteRule describes one transformation: matches(rule, operation) says whether the rule applies to an operation, and the rule replaces a matching operation with a short sequence of other operations. A rule does not know which gates are allowed at the end, so it is applied once at a time, and a basis decides when to stop.

The rules below are the ones the built-in bases use. They can also be used on their own.

RuleMatchesProduces
CanonicalRewritemost operationsone level of the standard decomposition, down to GateU and GateCX
FlattenContainersGateCall, Block and their inversesthe instructions they contain
SpecialAngleRewriteGateRX, GateRY, GateRZ by a multiple of $\pi/4$exact Clifford+T gates
ToZRotationRewriteGateRX, GateRYGateRZ between Clifford gates
ZYZRewriteGateU other than the identity, GateRXGateRZ and GateRY rotations
ToffoliToCliffordTRewriteGateCCXGateH, GateCX, GateT and GateTDG
GridSynthRewriteGateRZ with a numeric angleapproximate Clifford+T sequence (gridsynth)
SolovayKitaevRewriteGateRX, GateRY, GateRZ, GateU with numeric parametersapproximate Clifford+T sequence (Solovay-Kitaev)

Applying a rule directly

Pass a rule to decompose_step to see what it does to one operation:

# RX(θ) = H · RZ(θ) · H
decompose_step(GateRX(0.3); rule=ToZRotationRewrite())
1-qubit circuit with 3 instructions:
├── H @ q[1]
├── RZ(0.3) @ q[1]
└── H @ q[1]
# RY needs an extra S† ... S around the Hadamards
decompose_step(GateRY(0.3); rule=ToZRotationRewrite())
1-qubit circuit with 5 instructions:
├── S† @ q[1]
├── H @ q[1]
├── RZ(0.3) @ q[1]
├── H @ q[1]
└── S @ q[1]

SpecialAngleRewrite only matches angles that have an exact Clifford+T form. With only_cliffords=true it matches multiples of $\pi/2$ only, so the result contains no T gate:

# RZ(π/4) is T up to a global phase
decompose_step(GateRZ(π / 4); rule=SpecialAngleRewrite())
1-qubit circuit with 1 instruction:
└── T @ q[1]
# a generic angle, and T when only Clifford gates are allowed, are not matched
matches(SpecialAngleRewrite(), GateRZ(0.123)),
matches(SpecialAngleRewrite(only_cliffords=true), GateRZ(π / 4))
(false, false)

ZYZRewrite writes a single-qubit GateU as Euler rotations, leaving out the rotations by zero:

# U(θ, ϕ, λ) becomes RZ(λ), RY(θ), RZ(ϕ) in time order
decompose_step(GateU(0.1, 0.2, 0.3); rule=ZYZRewrite())
1-qubit circuit with 3 instructions:
├── RZ(0.3) @ q[1]
├── RY(0.1) @ q[1]
└── RZ(0.2) @ q[1]

ToffoliToCliffordTRewrite gives the standard decomposition of the Toffoli gate with seven T or T† gates and six CX gates:

decompose_step(GateCCX(); rule=ToffoliToCliffordTRewrite())
3-qubit circuit with 15 instructions:
├── H @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[2], q[3]
├── T† @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[3]
├── T @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[2], q[3]
├── T† @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[3]
├── T @ q[2]
├── T @ q[3]
├── H @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[2]
├── T @ q[1]
├── T† @ q[2]
└── CX @ q[1], q[2]

SolovayKitaevRewrite approximates an arbitrary single-qubit rotation. The first argument is the recursion depth: each level makes the approximation more precise and the sequence longer. The first use builds a table of short gate sequences (an $\varepsilon$-net) and caches it; net_max_points limits its size, which trades precision for the time it takes to build:

sk = SolovayKitaevRewrite(2; net_max_points=2000)
approx = decompose_step(GateRZ(0.123); rule=sk)

# the sequence uses only Clifford+T gates
length(approx), unique(opname.(getoperation.(approx)))
(158, ["T†", "H", "T", "S†", "S", "Z"])

ToZRotationRewrite, ToffoliToCliffordTRewrite and the GateRX rule of ZYZRewrite are exact. SpecialAngleRewrite and the GateU rule of ZYZRewrite are exact up to a global phase, and the approximate rules up to their tolerance and a global phase.

Applying a rule to an operation it does not match throws a DecompositionError. Check with matches first, or apply it to a whole circuit, as shown in Step-wise decomposition.

Rules as bases

A rule used as a basis is applied again and again, to the output too, until no instruction matches it any more. RuleBasis does that; passing the rule itself as basis is the same:

c = Circuit()
push!(c, GateRX(0.3), 1)
push!(c, GateRY(π / 2), 2)
push!(c, GateCCX(), 1, 2, 3)

# only RX and RY match; the Toffoli is left as it is
decompose(c; basis=ToZRotationRewrite())
3-qubit circuit with 9 instructions:
├── H @ q[1]
├── RZ(0.3) @ q[1]
├── H @ q[1]
├── S† @ q[2]
├── H @ q[2]
├── RZ(π/2) @ q[2]
├── H @ q[2]
├── S @ q[2]
└── C₂X @ q[1:2], q[3]
# RY(π/2) is rewritten exactly; RX(0.3) and the Toffoli do not match
decompose(c; basis=RuleBasis(SpecialAngleRewrite()))
3-qubit circuit with 3 instructions:
├── RX(0.3) @ q[1]
├── SY @ q[2]
└── C₂X @ q[1:2], q[3]

This is useful to apply one specific transformation and leave the rest of the circuit untouched. To combine several rules into a basis with a fixed target gate set, write a custom basis as described in Custom Decompositions.

Step-wise decomposition

decompose repeats decomposition steps until every operation is terminal in the basis. decompose_step performs a single step, which helps to see how an operation is broken down level by level. It takes an operation, an instruction or a circuit, and a rule keyword that defaults to CanonicalRewrite:

# one level: the Toffoli becomes H, CX, T and T† gates
step1 = decompose_step(GateCCX())
3-qubit circuit with 15 instructions:
├── H @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[2], q[3]
├── T† @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[3]
├── T @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[2], q[3]
├── T† @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[3]
├── T @ q[2]
├── T @ q[3]
├── H @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[2]
├── T @ q[1]
├── T† @ q[2]
└── CX @ q[1], q[2]
# the next level writes H, T and T† as GateU
step2 = decompose_step(step1)
3-qubit circuit with 15 instructions:
├── U(π/2,0,π) @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[2], q[3]
├── U(0,0,-1π/4) @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[3]
├── U(0,0,π/4) @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[2], q[3]
├── U(0,0,-1π/4) @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[3]
├── U(0,0,π/4) @ q[2]
├── U(0,0,π/4) @ q[3]
├── U(π/2,0,π) @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[2]
├── U(0,0,π/4) @ q[1]
├── U(0,0,-1π/4) @ q[2]
└── CX @ q[1], q[2]

On a circuit, decompose_step rewrites each instruction the rule matches and copies the others unchanged, so it can target one kind of operation:

# expand only the Toffoli gates, keep the rotations as they are
decompose_step(c; rule=ToffoliToCliffordTRewrite())
3-qubit circuit with 17 instructions:
├── RX(0.3) @ q[1]
├── RY(π/2) @ q[2]
├── H @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[2], q[3]
├── T† @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[3]
├── T @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[2], q[3]
├── T† @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[3]
├── T @ q[2]
├── T @ q[3]
├── H @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[2]
├── T @ q[1]
├── T† @ q[2]
└── CX @ q[1], q[2]

decompose_step! does the same, but appends the result to a circuit you already have instead of returning a new one. An operation is applied to the first qubits and bits:

acc = Circuit()

# append one Clifford+T step of a Toffoli, then the three CX of a SWAP,
# to the same circuit
decompose_step!(acc, GateCCX(); rule=ToffoliToCliffordTRewrite())
decompose_step!(acc, GateSWAP())
acc
3-qubit circuit with 18 instructions:
├── H @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[2], q[3]
├── T† @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[3]
├── T @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[2], q[3]
├── T† @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[3]
├── T @ q[2]
├── T @ q[3]
├── H @ q[3]
├── CX @ q[1], q[2]
├── T @ q[1]
├── T† @ q[2]
├── CX @ q[1], q[2]
├── CX @ q[1], q[2]
├── CX @ q[2], q[1]
└── CX @ q[1], q[2]

A rule implements the low-level method decompose_step!(builder, rule, op, qtargets, ctargets, ztargets), which appends the decomposition of op acting on the given targets to builder. See Custom Decompositions to write one.

Decomposition Iterators

For large circuits, you can iterate over decomposed instructions lazily without creating an intermediate circuit using eachdecomposed.

for inst in eachdecomposed(large_circuit, basis=QASMBasis())
    # Process instructions one by one
    println(inst)
end

Wrapping Decompositions

By default, decompose flattens the circuit. If you want to preserve the hierarchy by wrapping decomposed sequences into GateDecl or Block operations, use wrap=true.

# Preserves structure, wrapping decompositions in custom gate definitions
structured_circ = decompose(circuit, wrap=true)
