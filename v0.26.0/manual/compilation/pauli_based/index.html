Pauli-based Computation

In Pauli-based computation a circuit is expressed with two kinds of operations only: $\pi/8$ rotations about multi-qubit Pauli operators, and measurements of multi-qubit Pauli operators. This is the form used by lattice-surgery architectures for surface codes, where every logical operation is a Pauli product measurement.

pauli_based_computation performs this rewrite:

new_circuit = pauli_based_computation(circuit; basis=CliffordTBasis(), magic_states=false)
  • basis: the basis used to decompose the circuit into Clifford+T gates first. Measurements, resets and conditional Pauli operators are kept whole, whatever the basis.
  • magic_states: if true, each rotation is further replaced by the injection of a magic state.

The circuit is decomposed into Clifford+T, then every Clifford gate is moved to the end of the circuit. On the way, each T gate becomes an RPauli rotation of angle $\pm\pi/4$, and each measurement of a Pauli product (Measure, MeasureX, MeasureXX, MeasurePauli and the like) becomes a MeasurePauli. The Cliffords that end up after the last measurement do not change any outcome, so they are dropped. The new circuit gives the same measurement statistics as the original one, but not the same final state.

Example

Prepare a Bell pair, apply a T gate to qubit 2 and measure both qubits:

c = Circuit()
push!(c, GateH(), 1)
push!(c, GateCX(), 1, 2)
push!(c, GateT(), 2)
push!(c, Measure(), 1:2, 1:2)

pauli_based_computation(c)
2-qubit, 2-bit circuit with 3 instructions:
├── R("XZ", π / 4) @ q[1:2]
├── MP(X) @ q[1], c[1]
└── MP(XZ) @ q[1:2], c[2]

The Hadamard and CX gates before the T gate map $Z_2$ to $X_1 Z_2$, so the T gate becomes a $\pi/8$ rotation about $X_1 Z_2$ (RPauli with angle $\pi/4$). The same gates turn the measurements of $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ into measurements of $X_1$ and $X_1 Z_2$, written to the original bits.

Conditional operations and resets

An IfStatement whose body contains only Pauli operators, directly or inside a PauliString, Block, Parallel, Repeat or gate call, applies one Pauli product. It becomes one conditional PauliString, and the Clifford gates before it are moved past it as they are moved past a measurement. A conditional T or T† gate becomes a conditional RPauli rotation.

A reset (Reset, ResetX, ResetY) measures its qubit into a new bit, placed after the bits of the circuit, then applies a conditional Pauli that brings the qubit back to the reset state. The measure-and-reset operations do the same with the bit they are given.

c2 = Circuit()
push!(c2, GateH(), 1)
push!(c2, Measure(), 1, 1)
push!(c2, GateH(), 2)
push!(c2, IfStatement(GateZ(), bs"1"), 2, 1)
push!(c2, Reset(), 1)
push!(c2, Measure(), 1:2, 2:3)

pauli_based_computation(c2)
2-qubit, 4-bit circuit with 6 instructions:
├── MP(X) @ q[1], c[1]
├── IF(c==1) X @ q[2], condition[1]
├── MP(X) @ q[1], c[4]
├── IF(c==1) Z @ q[1], condition[4]
├── MP(X) @ q[1], c[2]
└── MP(X) @ q[2], c[3]

The Hadamard gate on qubit 2 turns the conditional $Z_2$ into a conditional $X_2$. The reset of qubit 1 measures $X_1$ into the new bit 4 and applies $Z_1$ when the outcome is 1.

Other conditional operations are refused with an error. After a conditional Clifford gate, such as a conditional Hadamard, the Clifford gates to move would depend on the condition bits, so the later rotations and measurements could not be computed before the circuit runs.

Magic-state injection

A $\pi/8$ rotation can itself be carried out with Pauli product measurements by consuming a magic state $T\ket{+}$, prepared with PrepareStateT. With magic_states=true every rotation is replaced in this way. This uses one extra ancilla qubit, two extra bits per rotation, and two bits per data qubit for the Pauli frame:

pauli_based_computation(c; magic_states=true)
3-qubit, 8-bit circuit with 8 instructions:
├── PrepareStateT @ q[3]
├── MP(XZZ) @ q[1:3], c[3]
├── IF(c==0) MP(X) @ q[3], c[4], condition[3]
├── IF(c==1) MP(Y) @ q[3], c[4], condition[3]
├── c[5] ⊻= c[4]
├── c[8] ⊻= c[4]
├── MP(X) @ q[1], c[1]
└── MP(XZ) @ q[1:2], c[2]

Reading the result line by line:

  1. PrepareStateT puts the ancilla (qubit 3) in the magic state.
  2. The first MeasurePauli measures the rotation axis $X_1 Z_2$ together with $Z$ on the ancilla.
  3. Depending on that outcome, the ancilla is measured in X or in Y. The two IfStatement lines implement this choice.
  4. The two Xor lines record the byproduct described below in the Pauli frame.
  5. The final measurements are the same as before.

When the second ancilla measurement gives 1, the rotation comes with an extra Pauli $X_1 Z_2$ on the data qubits. It is not corrected with gates. Instead, the product of all such Paulis is kept in classical bits, the Pauli frame, with one bit for the X part and one for the Z part of each data qubit. Later outcomes that the frame would flip are corrected in place with 3-bit Xor operations. Here both final measurements commute with $X_1 Z_2$, so no correction is needed.

With magic states a conditional Pauli is not applied either: it multiplies the frame when its condition holds. A conditional T or T† gate is refused, since a magic state cannot be injected conditionally.
